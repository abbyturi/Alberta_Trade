# Question 2 — October 12, 2026 legal-status correction and applied source register

**Do not use the existing CAD 6.62B partial HS6 screen as Canada's Section 338 tariff exposure.** It is not even a defensible lower bound because HS6 categories can contain uncovered U.S. tariff lines.

## Confirmed legal chronology

- Proclamations 11046, 11047 and 11048 imposed 50% duties on specified products, but **Proclamation 11056 postponed the effective date from August 19 to August 22, 2026** (three-day suspension only). Source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-24/pdf/2026-17294.pdf
- **Proclamation 11064 amended the alcoholic-beverage product scope**, separately from **Proclamation 11065**, which amended motor-vehicle scope; both changes effective September 15, 2026. The earlier interim ledger only represented 11065 additions/removals and therefore is incomplete. Source: https://www.usitc.gov/2026_hts_revision_19 and https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/429db0c
- USITC published **2026 HTS Revision 21 on October 9, 2026** with a CSV download: https://www.usitc.gov/2026_hts_revision_21. Direct download is blocked by the execution environment; the schedule is NOT bundled or verified at tariff-line level.
- CBP lists Chapter 99 headings 9903.03.12–9903.03.16 and identifies exceptions for Section 232-covered products and civil aircraft. Source: https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/4261d04
- Import prohibitions must remain separate from tariff exposure.
